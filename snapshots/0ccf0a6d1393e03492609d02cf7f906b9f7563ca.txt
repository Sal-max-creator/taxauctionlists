About Tax‑Forfeited Land
What is Tax-Forfeited Land?
Tax-forfeited land is property that the State of Minnesota takes ownership of when property taxes are not paid. Once forfeited, the county manages the property and may sell, lease, or transfer ownership according to state and local laws.
This is different from a mortgage foreclosure , which happens when mortgage payments are not made.
Learn more about what happens before forfeiture on the Property Tax Information page under Delinquent Property Taxes:
Go to Property Tax Information
Newly forfeited property is typically offered for sale at an initial public auction within six months of either the date of forfeiture or the date the property was vacated, whichever is later.
Property repurchased by the former owner or property withheld from sale by the Department of Natural Resources due to condemnation is excluded from the auction.
Proceeds from the sale are used to:
Pay administrative and sales expenses.
Pay canceled special assessments.
Distribute remaining funds as follows:
40% to the county
20% to the municipality
40% to local school districts
The county may also set aside up to 20% for parks.
If the sale brings in more than the amount owed, former owners and interested parties may claim the surplus proceeds.
Surplus Proceeds
If a property sells at the initial public auction for more than the amount owed, the former owner and other interested parties may be entitled to the surplus proceeds from the sale of tax-forfeited land:
The county will mail a notice and claim form to the former owner and other interested parties.
There is a six-month period after the sale to submit a claim.
Approved claims are paid after the six-month claim period ends.
Surplus claims only apply to initial public auctions&mdash;not later inventory sales.
Mineral Interests
Mineral interests are sold to the State of Minnesota for $50. If a former owner believes their mineral rights are worth more, they may submit a claim within six months of the sale. There is a six-month period to submit a claim.
Purchasing Tax‑Forfeited Land
Ways to Acquire Tax‑Forfeited Land
Repurchase by former owner or interested party
Former owners or other interested parties may repurchase tax-forfeited land before the initial public auction.
Contact us to request a repurchase application and payment calculation.
Complete the application and provide proof of ownership or other interest.
Submit payment in full by cashier's check, money order, or certified check.
Payment must be received by 4:30 p.m. on the business day before the sale.
Repurchase Costs
State deed tax - 0.0033% of the repurchase amount (minimum $1.65)
Repurchase fee - $250
State deed issuance fee - $25
County conservation fee - $5
County recording fee - $46
Well certificate (if required) - $50
Public Auction Sale
Newly tax-forfeited property is offered for sale to the highest bidder at an initial public auction within six months of the later of the date of forfeiture, or the date the property was vacated. The initial public auction is a two-part sale:
The first auction starts at the most recent estimated market value as the starting bid. The property is first offered for sale to the highest bidder. The starting bid is the property's most recent estimated market value.
If the property doesn't sell after 30 days, the starting bid is lowered to the amount owed in delinquent taxes, assessments, penalties, interest, and costs.
Sale or conveyance to local governments, government entities, or associations
Government agencies may acquire forfeited property for:
Public use (free of charge)
Public purpose (at fair market value or below, if used for housing or blight removal)
A property may be conveyed to a local government or homeowners association if they were entitled to it under a written agreement that did not occur before forfeiture.
Inventory public auction sale
If property remains unsold after the initial auction, it becomes part of the county's inventory and may later be sold at another public auction or offered over- the- counter.
The minimum bid is the appraised value plus any new special assessments.
The property can't be resold for less than the minimum bid unless reappraised and reoffered.
Private sale to adjoining owners
Certain parcels (such as small slivers of land or unbuildable lots) may be sold privately to the adjoining landowners if:
The parcel does not meet local zoning requirements
Combining it with a neighboring parcel would make better use of the land
Private sales are usually done through sealed bids to the highest bidder. If you own land next to a tax-forfeited parcel and believe it may qualify, contact us to request a review.
Current Sales
Notices of sales are posted on the Washington County website at least 30 days before a sale and published in the county newspaper. Sales may happen in person or online. Each listing will state the format.
