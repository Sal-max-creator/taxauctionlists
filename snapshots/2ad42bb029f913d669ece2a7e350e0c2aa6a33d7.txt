What is a Tax Deed?
Sumter County real estate taxes are due each year on November 1, payable through March 31 of the following year, with discounts allowed for early payments and a 3% penalty imposed beginning April 1. Items remaining unpaid by Mid-April are advertised in the local newspaper for three consecutive weeks in May in an effort to notify the property owners of outstanding tax liability.
According to Florida Statutes, on or before June 1, a tax certificate sale must be held. This is a public auction where a tax certificate is sold to the party who will pay the property owner&#8217;s tax and accept the lowest interest rate when redemption is made. A certificate is an interest-bearing lien, and the interest rate varies from 0% to 18%.
Certificates are sold on any items remaining unpaid at the time of the sale. Once a certificate is sold, the property owners may redeem the certificate by payment of taxes plus penalty, advertising, fees, and interest. If the certificate is not redeemed within a two-year time period, the certificate holder may begin proceedings for a tax deed sale.
Tax Deed Procedures
Tax Deed Application Process
The Tax Collector will file the tax deed application along with the tax certificate, and the Clerk shall review the documents and start the process for the tax deed sale.
The certificate holder is notified of the cost of advertising, mailing, and sheriff service required to be paid to the Clerk as required by statute.
Notice of tax deed sales is sent to the Sumter County Times for advertisement at least 30 days prior to the sale date and published for four (4) consecutive weeks.
The Sheriff posts the property at least 20 days prior to the date of sale, and notice is sent to all interested parties by certified mail.
The list of persons to be notified is furnished to the Clerk by the Tax Collector after the title search has been completed and filed with the Tax Collector.
Tax Deed Sale
The tax deed sale is a public auction, which is held on the front steps of the historic courthouse, and the land will be sold to the highest bidder.
The high bidder must post with the Clerk 5% of the bid or $200, whichever is greater, at the time of the sale, to be applied to the sale price at the time of full payment. You MUST have the funds for deposit at the time of sale.
If there are no bidders, the land shall be sold to the certificate holder who is required to pay the documentary stamp tax and recording fees immediately after the sale.
If the County makes the tax deed application and the property does not sell,then the Clerk shall place the property on the list of lands available for taxes.
Distribution of Sale Proceeds
If the property is purchased for an amount in excess of the minimum bid, the excess shall be distributed to governmental units that held liens against the property.
If, for some reason, the amount was not sufficient to satisfy the governmental liens, then the lien will survive the issuance of the tax deed.
All liens are to be satisfied prior to any excess funds being distributed to any other interested party or the titleholder.
The Clerk notifies all interested parties, and they have One Hundred twenty(120) days to file an affidavit to claim the funds. If excess overbid funds have not been claimed within 1 year after they became payable, the funds are considered “unclaimed property” and disposed of in the manner prescribed inCH. 717.117 Florida Statues.
Liens
Governmental liens & judgments survive the issuance of the tax deed and are satisfied to the fullest extent possible with any overbid monies from the sale. Liens of governmental units not satisfied in full survive the issuance of the tax deed. Should you have any questions concerning what liens & judgments survive the tax deed sale, consult an attorney; we can not advise you.
Surplus Affidavit
When making a claim, you must also provide proof of ID. If making a claim on funds for a deceased relative, you must provide proof with copies of probate work showing that you are the heir(s).

Frequently Asked Questions
If you have any questions, please contact our Official Records Division at (352) 569-6600 , and a Deputy Clerk will be able to assist you.
What is a tax deed sale?
A tax deed sale is a public auction where property is sold to the highest bidder to recover delinquent property taxes.
Who conducts the tax deed sale?
The clerk&#8217;s office conducts the sale via public auction.
Where is the tax deed sale held?
Tax deed sales are held at the Sumter County Courthouse &#8211; Historic Wing &#8211; Third Floor &#8211; Courtroom F
When is the tax deed sale held?
Tax Deed Sales are generally held several times per year on Thursdays at 11:00 AM. Check with our office for dates and times.
Are the tax deed sales advertised in a newspaper?
Yes. Tax deed sales are advertised in the Sumter County Times.
Do I have to register with the Clerks office before the sale?
Yes. In order to participate in the sale, bidders must sign in on the day of the sale.
Is there a deposit required to bid on property at a tax deed sale?
Yes. A deposit of $200 or 5% of the winning bid, whichever is greater, is required for each item you anticipate winning at the sale. Deposits may be in the form of cash, cashier’s check, or money order.
What if I need additional information regarding a property being sold at a tax deed sale?
Through the Events Calendar, all pending sales will list the owner&#8217;s name, parcel identification number, and opening bid. If you need additional information, you may come into our office to view the file.
How do I find out if there are any liens or encumbrances on a property being sold at a tax deed sale?
An ownership and encumbrance report for each certificate is found in the tax deed file held by the clerk&#8217;s office. However, the Clerk&#8217;s office is not responsible for any discrepancies or omissions on this report, and it is the responsibility of the buyer to research the property that he/she is interested in purchasing.
Tax Deeds are sold as is on a &#8220;Buyer Beware&#8221; basis. Properties may be subject to additional taxes and/or liens.
If I am the highest bidder at a sale, how long do I have to pay for the property?
If you are the successful bidder for property at a tax deed sale, full payment must be received by the Clerk’s office within 24 hours after the time of sale. Accepted forms of payment are cash, cashier’s check, or money order.
What if I am the highest bidder and I then choose not to purchase the property for any reason?
If full payment is not made within 24 hours after the time of the sale, your deposit will be forfeited. Additionally, if you are the high bidder and refuse to honor your bid for any reason, you may be barred from participating in Sumter County’s tax deed sales.
Where can I find information regarding the sale of tax certificates?
Any information regarding the sale of tax certificates must be obtained from:
Sumter County Tax Collector’s Office
220 E McCollum Ave
Bushnell, FL
(352) 569-6740
What does it mean for an owner to redeem a tax deed property?
To redeem a tax deed property means that the owner pays all current and delinquent taxes to the Tax Collector’s office, and the property will no longer be going to sale.
How do I know if a property has been redeemed before the sale date?
The status of properties scheduled for sale will be periodically updated on our site. Every effort is made to keep this information current. However, to check for last minute redemption&#8217;s, you may contact the Tax Deed Clerk at (352) 569-6600 .
What happens to property that is not purchased at the tax deed sale?
If a property goes up for bid at a tax deed sale and is not purchased, then the applicant holding the tax certificate is required to take the property. If the applicant is Sumter County, then the land will go onto the Lands Available list.
Land is available for purchase from the Lands Available List after it has been listed for 90 days. The purchase price is the opening bid plus any taxes and interest that have accrued on the property since it was placed on the list.
