2026 Tax Foreclosure Auction Information
The 2026 tax foreclosure auction was held on Friday, June 26, 2026 in the Riley County Public Works building, in the lower level training room, 6215 Tuttle Creek Boulevard.
Please sign up for the Tax Sales Information: Notify Me to receive email/text updates.
2026 Tax Auction Results
Property Owners
If your property is in the current tax foreclosure case you will not be able to make an online payment.
Payment will need to be made in the Riley County Treasurer&apos;s Office before 5:00 pm Monday through Thursday and before 12:00 noon on Friday. Any payment made to the Riley County Treasurer before the court costs have been paid will be held and not applied to the taxes until that check has been received.
At fourteen (14) days prior to the auction, all payments must be made in the form of certified funds (cash, cashier&apos;s check or money order). All tax payments must be made out to "Riley County Treasurer," and the court cost check must be made out to the "Clerk of the District Court."
If the property goes to auction and sells for more than the amount of the County&apos;s total lien, the excess proceeds will be distributed upon further order of the Court and pursuant to K.S.A. 79-2803.
If the property sells for less than the amount of the County&apos;s total lien, ALL liens against the property are extinguished.
Registration Form
Before the Auction
It is your responsibility to acquire information about the properties that interest you to determine if they are suitable for your use. You may want to:
Sign up for the "Notify Me" service
Check to see if the property has been redeemed
Determine the location and type of property
Check with the Treasurer&apos;s Office for current tax bill amount
Check with the city or County Planning & Development for zoning and building limitations
Check with the County Appraiser for assessed value and current tax rates
Check with the Register of Deeds for easements and restrictive covenants
Check with the Clerk&apos;s Office to see if the property has special assessments
Contact a local title company for information concerning the availability of title insurance
Riley County makes no assurances that you can obtain title insurance.
The current owner(s) may redeem their property at any time prior to the day of sale. These properties will not be offered for sale.
No person may register as a bidder if that person is the record owner of any real estate located in Riley County, Kansas, upon which there are any delinquent ad valorem property taxes owed.
THE PERSON WHO HAS REGISTERED TO BID MUST BE THE PERSON BIDDING AT THE AUCTION. No proxy bidding will be allowed.
The registration deadline is 5:00 pm one week prior to the date of the Auction.
The Auction
The properties will be sold by public auction to the highest qualified bidder. There is no minimum bid. Bidders are reminded that the auctioneer's acceptance of the highest bid constitutes an enforceable contract. DO NOT BID IF YOU ARE NOT ABSOLUTELY CERTAIN ABOUT WHAT YOU ARE PURCHASING. Once your bid has been accepted by the auctioneer, you cannot "return" the property or get a refund.
If a tract purchased at the sale is transferred, sold, given or otherwise conveyed to any person who did have a statutory right to redeem such tract prior to such sale, within 10 years from the date of the auction, such person shall be liable for an amount equal to the original judgment lien and interest thereon from the date of the public auction (personal judgment).
Each successful bidder will be required to sign an affidavit which shall be filed with the Clerk of the District Court stating the purchase was not directly or indirectly made for any of the following:
Any person having the statutory right to redeem such real estate prior to the sale, pursuant to the provisions of K.S.A. 79-2803, as amended, except that this paragraph shall not prohibit sale to any person or such person's assignee who held an interest in such real estate as mortgagee of record at the time of the sale;
Any parent, grandparent, child, grandchild, spouse, sibling, and trustee or trust beneficiary of any person enumerated in paragraph (1); and
Any current or former stockholder, current officer or director of a corporation holding title to such real estate, or any person having a relationship enumerated in paragraph (2) to such stockholder, officer or director.
A false swearing/affirmation may constitute a felony offense.
Properties will be sold by item number, legal description and by county tax identification number. If the property is subject to a federal tax lien or federal judgment lien such lien will be so designated when it is sold.
Properties are sold "as is." There are no warranties and t he County makes no assurances that you can obtain title insurance.
Payment and Fees
All properties must be paid for by 12:00 noon on the day of the sale. Checks are accepted as well as debit or credit cards, a 2.5% processing fee will be added when using debit or credit cards. Checks should be made payable to the Clerk of the District Court. No cash will be accepted. The buyer will receive a receipt upon payment.
State law requires that a $21 recording fee be collected at the time payment is made, and there is an additional charge of $17 per page after the first page. The County Counselor&apos;s staff will estimate these charges. Overcharges will be returned to the buyer. Undercharges must be paid prior to recording.
After the Auction
WRAPPING UP THE CASE : As soon as practicable after the auction, our office will file the remainder of the necessary documents with the District Court. We then file a Motion requesting the Court to approve and confirm the sale and set a date for the final hearing.
After the sale is confirmed by the Court, the Director's Deeds will be prepared and recorded with the Register of Deeds Office.
It may take up to 90 days to receive your recorded deed to the property. This waiting period allows all funds to clear, the recording of deeds and the settlement of any disputed sales (challenges) in court.
TAKING POSSESSION : Deeds for properties subject to a federal lien will not be issued until the expiration of the federal redemption period, and then only if there has been no redemption. The redemption period for a federal tax lien is 120 days from the date of sale.
You are NOT the owner of the property until such time as the sale is confirmed. Until the sale is confirmed, you do not have a right to collect rent or require the owner to leave the premises. You do not have the right to start removing, demolishing, cleaning up or otherwise altering the property. You do not have the right to change the locks on the property. Once the sale is confirmed with the Court, then you have an interest in the property, subject to receipt of your Director's Deed.
OCCUPANTS : If there are occupants of the property at the time you receive your deed, you will be required to obtain a court order directing the sheriff to evict the occupants. The sheriff cannot act without a court order. The County is not responsible for obtaining possession for you or for your expenses to obtain possession.
TAXES : You will be responsible for any taxes, including specials and assessments (sewer, water, paving, etc.), which are not included in the judgment, including the full amount of taxes assessed against the property for the calendar year in which the auction is held. Keep in mind you are responsible for the special assessments until they expire or until you sell the property. Any assessments for demolition, weed cutting, or nuisance removal that occur prior to the date of judgment will be abated.
LIENS/ENCUMBRANCES: All other liens of record will be extinguished upon confirmation of the sale; however, covenants, restrictions and easements of record are not extinguished, and they buyer takes the property subject to those encumbrances.
PERSONAL PROPERTY : The buyer takes title to the real estate, and that which is typically understood to be real estate, only. A buyer does not get title to any personal property on the purchased property. You need to consult a lawyer in order to determine how to dispose of any personal property found on the premises.
CONDEMNATION/CODE VIOLATIONS : The buyer takes title subject to any pending actions to condemn the property for code violations or any environmental enforcement proceedings. It is up to you to research whether the property you are purchasing is part of such actions. You may find that the property has been demolished since the last time you inspected it or that a demolition is soon to occur. This is not a basis that allows you to have the sale set aside.
PROPERTY INSURANCE : During the time period after the sale and up to confirmation, you may not have an insurable interest. Therefore, if the property is destroyed by fire or other casualty before you are able to get insurance, it is not a basis to set aside the sale. You need to consult with your insurance agent as to when you have sufficient interest for your insurance company to issue a policy protecting the property. This is a risk of purchasing tax foreclosure property.
TITLE INSURANCE: Riley County makes no assurances that you can obtain title insurance guaranteeing the marketability of the property purchased at the tax sale, nor title insurance for a bank to which you may grant a mortgage, nor title insurance to a subsequent buyer from you. This is not a basis that allows you to have the sale set aside.
The County will not furnish any abstract of title, any commitments to insure from any title insurance company, nor will the County furnish any title evidence.
If the property purchased at the sale is transferred, sold, given, or otherwise conveyed to any person who had a statutory right to redeem prior to the sale, within ten (10) years of the date of this auction, such person shall be liable for an amount equal to the original judgment lien and interest thereon from the date of sale as required by K.S.A. 79 2804g(c).
SETTING ASIDE THE SALE : Even though there are no redemption rights, K.S.A. 79-2804b states that for twelve (12) months after the date the sale is confirmed by the court, a legal challenge may still be made questioning the foreclosure procedures used by the County. If such a challenge is successful, the property could revert back to the original owner, in which case the purchase price would be refunded to you. The twelve-month limitation does not apply to efforts based upon inadequate service of process; challenges due to lack of notice can persist after 12 months.
