Tax Deed Process
The manner in which delinquent taxes are collected is prescribed by Florida Statute 197 and Florida Administrative Code 12D-13.060 and is briefly outlined below:
Citrus County real estate taxes are due by November 1 each year, payable through March 31 of the following year.
On or before June 1, a Tax Certificate Auction must be held. This is a public auction held by the Tax Collector where a tax certificate is sold to the party who will pay the property owners' tax and accept the lowest interest rate. For more information on Tax Certificate sales, please visit the Citrus County Tax Collector website .
When a certificate is sold, the property owner can redeem the certificate by payment of the taxes plus penalty, fees and interest. Properties can be redeemed any time before the clerk has received full payment for the Tax Deed. Please contact the Tax Collector's Office for redemption information (352) 341-6500.
If the certificate is not redeemed within a two-year time period, the certificate holder may contact the Tax Collector&apos;s office to begin proceedings for a tax deed auction.
The Tax Collector will deliver the tax deed application to the Clerk of the Circuit Court and Comptroller to begin the process for a tax deed auction.
The opening bid includes past due taxes, plus the costs required to bring the property to sale and one-half of the assessed value if homestead exempt pursuant to F.S. 197.502(6)(c).
The Clerk of the Circuit Court and Comptroller cannot guarantee clear title to the properties. All properties are sold as is and conveyed by a Tax Deed from the Clerk. Mobile homes are not included on the Tax Deed. Transfer of the mobile home title is the responsibility of the Purchaser. It is the bidder's responsibility to search the title for any liens that may be recorded against the property. Governmental liens and judgments will survive the Tax Deed sale.
Tax Deed Auction
The tax deed auction is conducted electronically by the Clerk of the Circuit Court and Comptroller on the Real Auction website and is open to the public. To become a bidder, visit the Real Auction website and click Register. Training links and FAQ's are also available on their site for the auction process.
Prior to the auction, bidders shall post with the Clerk a deposit of $200, or 5% of their maximum bid, whichever is greater, for each property in which they plan to bid. Wire transfers, cashier's checks, money orders, and cash are acceptable deposit methods and must be settled to the bidder account by 4:00 pm on the day prior to the sale. Please note: wire transfers may take 1-2 business days to credit the Clerk&apos;s bank account. It is the bidder&apos;s responsibility to initiate the wire early enough for the funds to credit the Clerk&apos;s account within the time specified. When initiating a wire deposit a $5.00 wire transfer fee must be included with each transaction.
Successful bidders are required to make full payment to the Clerk of the Circuit Court and Comptroller by wire transfer, cashier's check, money orders, cash, or payment by deposit via the bidder's online Real Auction account within 24 hours of the advertised auction time. Failure to do so will result in the forfeiture of your bid deposit as well as being banned from future Tax Deed auctions. Pursuant to Florida Statutes, Section 197.542(1), "the Clerk may refuse to recognize the bid of any person who has previously bid and refused, for any reason, to honor such bid".
Auction payments must be made by wire transfer, pay from deposit, or in person with certified funds at the Citrus County Courthouse, 110 N. Apopka Avenue, Inverness, FL 34450. Please call (352) 341-6478 if you have any questions.
Bidders with cash transactions over $10,000.00 will be required to fill out IRS form 8300 .
Deeds are prepared and recorded using the name provided by the bidder on the Real Auction website under the name on the title. Please verify this information at the time of full payment to ensure that it is correct. It is the bidder's responsibility to make any corrections after the deed has been recorded.
If the certificate holder is not the high bidder, they are reimbursed their investment, which is the opening bid. If there is no bid on the property, the certificate holder shall pay recording fees and documentary stamp tax before a tax deed will be prepared and recorded.
Tax certificates owned by the County, not sold at the auction, are placed on the List of Lands Available for Taxes.
List of Lands Available
The list of Lands Available for taxes shows properties that were offered at a Tax Deed sale but were not purchased. Ninety (90) days after the sale, any person or governmental unit may purchase the land for the opening bid, plus the omitted years' taxes.
List of Lands Available inquiries should be sent to TaxDeeds@CitrusClerk.org . Please include the Case # and Certificate # for each case you are inquiring about. It could take up to two weeks to calculate the most up-to-date purchase price.
The purchase price includes the opening bid at the time of the auction, current year's taxes, interest, recording fees and documentary stamps. The purchase price is non-negotiable.
Acceptable forms of payments are certified check, cashier&apos;s check or money order. Properties are sold on a first come first serve basis. When funds are received to purchase a property from the list of Land Available for Taxes, a Tax Deed will be prepared and recorded.
Tax Deeds Surplus Funds
When a property is sold at a tax deed sale, the opening bid pays the delinquent taxes and the costs of bringing the property to auction. Surplus funds over the opening bid are held by the Clerk of the Circuit Court and Comptroller for the benefit of the parties listed in the Tax Collector's property information report, except for contiguous property owners.
Lienholders have 120 days from the date of the Notice of Surplus Funds to submit a notarized claim to the Clerk of the Circuit Court and Comptroller. Claimants other than the property owner, who fail to file a proper and timely claim, are barred from receiving any disbursement of the surplus funds.
Claims may be submitted by mail, email, or hand delivery. Once the 120-day claim window has closed, claims will be reviewed and processed according to priorities set forth in F.S. &sect;197.582, which can take up to 90 days. If any documentation received by email is not legible, our office may request copies be submitted by U.S. Mail.
Click HERE to download the Affidavit of Claim to Surplus Proceeds of a Tax Deed Sale.
All claimants must submit a legible copy of a valid government issued ID and W9 (a W-8BEN completed is needed for out of country claimants). All businesses and corporations are required to submit their annual filing documentation from the Secretary of State along with all relevant claims. Claims received without these documents will not be processed.
If no claims are received within one year from the date of the Notice of Surplus Funds, the balance is reported as Unclaimed Funds.
Visit Florida's Treasure Hunt website to search for unclaimed funds held by the State of Florida.
