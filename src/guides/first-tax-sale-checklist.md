---
title: Preparing for your first tax sale
description: A step-by-step checklist from picking a county to sale day.
order: 4
---

Your first tax sale goes best if you pick one county, learn its rules cold, and show up with a short list of properties you've already researched.

## A month or more before

- **Pick a county.** Start near you or in a state whose rules you understand. Use the [calendar](/calendar/) to find a sale date that gives you time to prepare.
- **Read the county's terms of sale.** Look for the deposit, payment deadline, accepted payment methods, bidding method and any fees.
- **Find out how the sale runs.** In person, online, or both. Online sales usually require you to register on an auction site well before the sale.

## Two to four weeks before

- **Register.** Many counties close registration days before the sale and may need a tax form (often a W-9) and an ID.
- **Get the list.** Download the county's list of properties when it's published. Expect it to shrink as owners pay.
- **Set a budget.** Decide how much you'll spend in total and the most you'll bid on each property.

## One to two weeks before

- **Research your shortlist.** Look at each property on the county's parcel map, assessor records and street view. See [Researching a property before you bid](/guides/researching-a-property/).
- **Arrange funds.** If the county wants a wire or certified funds, set that up with your bank ahead of time.

## Sale day

- Re-check the list. Properties get pulled up to the last minute.
- Bid only on what you researched. Auctions move fast, and it's easy to overpay.
- Pay on time. Missing the deadline usually costs you your deposit.

## After the sale

- Keep every receipt and certificate.
- Put redemption and filing deadlines on your calendar.
- For deeds, record your deed if the county doesn't do it for you, and get insurance in place.
