---
title: Online vs. in-person tax auctions
description: How each format works, and what changes for you as a bidder.
order: 7
---

More counties now run tax sales online, but plenty still hold them in a courthouse or county building. The rules for each format differ, so check before you plan.

## In-person sales

- Usually held at a set time and place, often a courthouse.
- You may need to register on site or ahead of time, and bring ID and a deposit.
- Bidding is out loud or by bidder card, and it moves fast.
- Payment is often due the same day, by certified funds.

In-person sales favor people who live nearby, which can mean fewer bidders.

## Online sales

- Run on a county site or a private auction platform the county hires.
- Registration closes ahead of the sale and usually requires a deposit, a tax form and sometimes proof of funds.
- Some sales let you place bids ahead of time, others run live, and some run batches of properties on a timer.
- Platforms charge their own fees in some counties. Read the platform's rules and the county's terms.

Online sales attract bidders from everywhere, which often means more competition and lower returns.

## Which is better?

Neither. Pick the county, then learn its format. If you're new, an in-person sale lets you watch how it works. Just go once without bidding.
