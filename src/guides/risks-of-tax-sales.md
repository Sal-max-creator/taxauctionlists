---
title: The real risks of buying at tax sales
description: What can go wrong, and how careful buyers limit it.
order: 9
---

Tax sales can work out well, but they aren't guaranteed returns. These are the risks that cost buyers money most often.

## Buying a property you can't use

Landlocked lots, drainage ditches, slivers between houses, condemned buildings and polluted land show up at every sale. Research every parcel before you bid.

## Overbidding

Competitive online sales can push lien interest rates down to almost nothing, or deed prices up to market value without the protections of a normal purchase. Set your limit before the sale.

## Title problems

Many tax deeds aren't insurable right away. Clearing title can mean a court case that takes months and costs money.

## Surviving debts

Some liens and debts can survive a tax sale. What survives depends on the state and the type of sale.

## Redemption and timing

Your money may be tied up for the whole redemption period. Bankruptcy can freeze things longer.

## Procedure mistakes

Missing a deadline to apply for a deed, skipping a required notice, or paying late can wipe out your investment.

## Occupied properties

You may need to go through an eviction or similar process to take possession.

## Changing rules

States change tax sale laws, and court decisions can change how surplus money is handled. What worked last year may not work this year.

The way to manage these risks is unglamorous: pick a county, read its rules, research every parcel, keep a budget, and talk to a local attorney before you take on anything big.
